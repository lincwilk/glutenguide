# Draft food-information and review principles

This is a planning document, not a clinical or legal standard. It must be reviewed with qualified celiac-disease / food-safety expertise before any real-world safety status is assigned.

## Never promise zero risk

No badge, review, document review or customer report can guarantee that an individual meal is free from gluten or cross-contact.

## Separate information types

1. **Public information found** — a public page or listing contains a relevant claim, but the venue has not directly confirmed it to GlutenGuide. Record the exact source and date checked. Do not label it restaurant-reported solely because the source is the venue’s website.
2. **Restaurant-reported** — the venue has directly supplied or confirmed the information to GlutenGuide. Record who confirmed it, the statement, source, and date.
3. **Evidence reviewed** — specific documents or procedures were examined against published criteria by an identified reviewer. Record the scope, reviewer role, evidence date, gaps, findings, and next review date. This is not a guarantee.
4. **Limited information** — information is missing, stale, contradictory, or inadequate to assess the listed practices.
5. **Community experience** — a customer report of their own interaction or visit. It is not proof of kitchen practices or food composition.

## Evidence and review workflow (to design)

- Ask for documented ingredient and allergen procedures.
- Ask how equipment, utensils, preparation surfaces, storage and fryers are handled.
- Record staff training and how ingredient changes are communicated.
- Define what evidence is required, who may review it, and how frequently it expires.
- Revisit status if a restaurant changes ownership, menu, process or relevant staff practices.
- Allow restaurants and users to report errors; keep an audit trail of changes.
- Clearly distinguish document review from an on-site inspection or third-party certification.
- Do not assign a status based only on a star rating, paid subscription or unverified crowd report.

## Community moderation

- Label comments as personal experiences.
- Disallow claims that symptoms alone prove gluten exposure.
- Provide reporting and appeal paths for disputed reviews.
- Avoid collecting medical information that is not necessary for the product.
- Before launch, establish privacy terms, retention rules and moderation procedures.

## Before real-world launch

Consult qualified food-safety professionals and relevant celiac organizations. Check applicable US federal, Illinois and Chicago rules; obtain appropriate legal and privacy advice. Do not market the platform as a certification authority unless a valid program and qualified governance are in place.
